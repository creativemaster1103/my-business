# 90-day creative diversity audit (format) — Hoolest Performance Technologies — 2026-Q3

## Executive summary

Hoolest is running a wide format mix and a narrow spend mix, and the gap between those two things is the whole story of this quarter. Thirty-nine distinct formats show up across the 568 tagged ads that delivered spend between June 10 and September 8. But three of them, Authority Figure at 26.1 percent of spend, Educational at 25.2 percent and Founder Ads at 23.5 percent, absorb three quarters of the money, and they are not three separate things. They overlap heavily. Twelve of the 46 Founder Ads are also tagged Authority Figure and 24 of them are also tagged Educational. What looks like a diverse account from the tag list is, at the money level, one repeated move: a credible man explains the mechanism. Verified from the Q3 format pull against total account spend of $1,045,684.92.

The dominant format by spend is Authority Figure, and it is not the one earning its keep. Authority Figure took $272,413.78 and returned 1.30 ROAS on 1,905 purchases, which is below the account's own 1.33. Founder Ads took $245,659.79 and returned 1.24. Educational took $263,844.06 and returned 1.24. Meanwhile the formats the account barely funds are the ones beating it. Comment Response, brand new this quarter, took $33,249.81 across 13 ads and returned 1.49. Comparison took $73,581.82 across 9 ads and returned 1.46 at a $126.21 CPA, the cheapest acquisition of any format with real spend behind it. Offer Based returned 1.46 on $95,905.64. That is the finding a creative director should act on: the account's cheapest purchases come from formats holding 2.3 percent and 1.6 percent of the tagged ad count. Verified from tag-filtered aggregate pulls, each with its own denominator stated in section two.

Three formats fell out of the mix entirely since the prior 90 days. Stitch Hooks is the loud one. In the March 12 to June 9 window a single Stitch Hooks ad carried $16,540.98, or 2.3 percent of that quarter's $733,915.93. This quarter it is zero. Billboard went from 3 ads and $3,649.10 to zero, and Social Interface from 1 ad and $446.65 to zero. Against that, twelve formats arrived that were not there before, led by Comment Response, and the account added nine net formats overall, going from 30 to 39. So the account is broadening, but it is broadening in the tail while the head stays put.

The gap most worth filling is the reaction lane, and the reason is sitting in the competitor library. Pulsetto, the rival that out-spends and out-covers Hoolest on awareness, has built essentially its entire tagged paid library on one format: 130 of 133 tagged ads, or 97.7 percent, are Reaction. Hoolest runs Reaction at 11 ads and $10,030.34, which is 1.0 percent of spend. A competitor has bet everything on a format Hoolest treats as a rounding error, and Hoolest has the one product asset that makes the format work better for it than for anyone else. The brand's own USP is the felt sensation, "the only vagus nerve device you can feel working." Reaction is the format built to show a felt sensation happening on someone's face. That is the lead recommendation for next quarter's test slate.

One more thing the reader needs before section two. The brand states that "Yapper Founder ads" are its best-performing format and that it prioritizes authenticity over polished ads. The Yapper tag exists in the format taxonomy. Across 568 tagged ads this quarter and 159 last quarter, it appears zero times. What the founder work actually carries is 3D graphics floating around Nick's head, a studio microphone, and in two cases an AI voiceover. The account's stated creative philosophy and the account's tagged creative reality have drifted apart, and that drift is worth a conversation before the next round is briefed.

### Authority Figure

- Count: 44 ads, 7.7 percent of 568 tagged ads
- Spend: $272,413.78, 26.1 percent of account spend
- ROAS: 1.30, against an account ROAS of 1.33
- CPA: $143.00, against an account CPA of $151.11
- CPM: $40.90 and CTR 3.06 percent, against $32.50 and 2.23 percent
- Purchases: 1,905 on $353,492.39 of revenue, AOV $185.56
- Hook rate and hold rate: data-limited at the format level. Named ads below carry their own.
- Trajectory: down. 35 ads and 22.0 percent of count last quarter at $197,018.52 and 26.8 percent of spend. Spend share flat, count share cut by two thirds.

Authority Figure is the account's largest spend bucket and it is doing the brand's credibility work. This is the format where a credentialed person vouches for the mechanism, and for a category where the dominant objection is "is this real or is it placebo," that job matters. The clearest example is HPT001, the Dr. Ryan iteration, which took $50,774.47 on its own. It opens on a young woman with wavy hair holding a black device behind her ear, looking genuinely startled, with the text hook "POV: You hacked your way out of fight or flight" and the spoken line "Oh my gosh! Chills all over my body, but then also the ear. Ooh, wow." A functional neurologist then explains the science of stress recovery. It returned 1.24 ROAS at a $143.43 CPA with a 26.12 percent hook rate and a 4.81 percent hold rate.

The format is performing at the account's own bar, not above it. A 1.30 ROAS against a 1.33 account ROAS means the largest single line of spend is diluting rather than lifting. The CPA is genuinely good at $143.00 against $151.11, and the CTR of 3.06 percent well beats the 2.23 percent account figure, so the format is earning clicks and buying purchases efficiently. What it is not doing is producing the high-value basket. At $185.56 the Authority Figure AOV sits under the $201.29 account AOV and well under static's $237.20. People click, they buy, they buy small.

The trajectory is the interesting part. Count share fell from 22.0 percent to 7.7 percent while spend share held at roughly a quarter. The account is running far fewer Authority Figure ads and putting the same money behind them, which means concentration. Five of the 44 Authority Figure ads are also tagged Comparison and those five carry $69,700.30, so a quarter of the Authority Figure money is really the founder ranking competitors on camera. Twelve are also tagged Founder Ads. Strip the overlap and the standalone third-party-expert lane is thinner than 26.1 percent makes it look. That is a concentration risk worth naming: the brand's biggest spend bucket is a small number of ads leaning on two people.

Educational is the account's teaching lane and it is the format that grew the most in absolute dollars this quarter, adding roughly $171,700 of spend. It is also the most entangled format in the account. Of the 102 Educational ads, 24 are also Founder Ads carrying $127,763.33 and 23 are also Authority Figure carrying $94,392.00. Only a minority of Educational spend is a standalone teaching ad. Sixteen are B-roll mashup plus Voiceover and five are Green Screen, which is where the genuinely different-looking Educational work lives.

Performance is honest but unspectacular. A 1.24 ROAS against 1.33 with a CPA right at the account line says the format is buying purchases at the going rate and no better. Its awareness split explains why it exists: 72 of 102 Educational ads are tagged Problem Aware carrying $154,733.58, which is 58.6 percent of the format's spend. Educational is the account's cold-traffic engine, the format that takes someone who knows they feel wired and teaches them that a vagus nerve exists and can be reached. Judged as acquisition rather than as efficiency, 1.24 on $263,844.06 pointed at problem-aware buyers is a defensible trade.

Where it is being used in a flat conventional way is the container. Educational is doing its work almost entirely as a person talking to camera about a mechanism, either the founder or the doctor. The five Green Screen executions at $10,140.12 and the sixteen B-roll and voiceover executions at $15,216.60 are the only meaningfully different-looking Educational shapes in the account, and together they are 9.6 percent of Educational spend. There is a whole family of educational containers the account has not touched: the diagram, the animation, the whiteboard, the screen recording, the physical prop demonstration held by nobody. That is not a message problem, it is a container problem, and containers are Tier 1.

This is the format the brand believes in most and it is the one carrying the widest spread between its best and worst executions. Founder Ads more than doubled its money this quarter and lands at 1.24 ROAS with a CPA above the account line, which on its face is the account's weakest large format. But the spread inside it is enormous. HPT025, the founder ranking competitors, returned 1.82 ROAS on $35,222.59 at a $101.51 CPA, the single best return of any ad above $30,000 in the window. HPT022, the founder answering objections, returned 1.09 on $45,501.98 at a $175.68 CPA. Same person, same format tag, same quarter, and a 67 percent swing in ROAS.

What separates them is visible in the media. HPT025 opens on a man in a black polo speaking into a microphone with animated 3D graphics of wearable devices floating around his head, and the spoken hook is a question the viewer already has: "Should you get the VeRelief or one of these other brands?" It hooks at 32.85 percent and holds at 8.79 percent with an 81.78 percent first-frame retention. HPT022 opens on a close-up of hands clicking a device, with the line "I invented this device that uses vagus nerve stimulation to stop a panic attack in 30 seconds." It hooks at 26.17 percent on a $66.01 CPM. The winner is the one that asks the viewer's question back to them. The loser is the one that asserts a credential.

Now the thing the brand needs to hear. Hoolest states that "Yapper Founder ads" are its best format and that it prioritizes authenticity over polished ads. The Yapper tag in the format taxonomy describes someone picking up a phone and talking in a raw, unpolished, uninterrupted stream with no professional setup, no meaningful edit pattern and no graphics. Zero of the 46 Founder Ads this quarter carry that tag, and zero of last quarter's 24 did either. What they carry instead is 3D motion graphics, a studio microphone, and in the case of HPT052 an AI voiceover. HPT052 is worth noting on its own: it took $34,027.61, returned 1.55 ROAS at a $117.74 CPA, and posted a 52.43 percent hook rate and a 28.25 percent hold rate, the strongest attention numbers of any high-spend ad in the account. The brand's intake says AI ads were tried and did not work. This one is working. Both of those facts are stated by the brand and verified in the account respectively, and they do not agree.

## Open loops

**1.
