# Hoolest Performance Technologies — brand lens

This is the brand's own tribal knowledge, loaded on top of the generic method on every creative
output. It is a **seed**, not a finished lens: the Phase-0 brand intake was never run, so most of
what a team would normally tell Parker directly is still missing. What is here came from the
team's existing automations repo and from the Q3 audits. `/refresh-context` and `/self-improve`
add to it as the brand teaches Parker more.

Every line is marked `stated` (the brand told us) or `verified` (the data confirms it).

## Voice and language

- The brand reads **direct, science-grounded and earnest** — mechanical, systems-level language
  rather than soft wellness tropes. No-BS over hype. `verified` (Parker brand-context analysis of
  live ad copy, 2026-09-08)
- Live ad copy leans on **mechanism framing** — "reset button", "off switch", "your nervous system
  is basically broken" — and explicitly rejects the meditation/breathwork register
  ("while everyone tells you to *just breathe*"). `verified` (top-spend ad copy, 2026-Q3)
- The brand positions against the $30,000 hospital machine as the credibility anchor.
  `verified` (top-spend ad, 2026-Q3)

## Claims and compliance constraints

The full gate is in `CLAUDE.md` under **Brand hard rules** and it outranks everything. In short:
no diagnosable condition as something the product acts on; never suggest replacing or reducing a
medication; no unsourced numbers; nothing verbatim from a competitor; no claim that assumes a
rival's mechanism; price live from Shopify. `stated` (team's own brief)

Angles that never transfer to a $399 non-prescription device: prescription/medication
replacement, named-condition treatment claims, ingestible dosing, subscription-only offer
mechanics. `stated` (`config/competitors.yml`)

## Do's and don'ts

- **Four distinct hook mechanisms per brief.** Never relaxed. `stated`
- **Longevity is the competitor-quality bar**: an ad must have run 30+ days to be worth swiping.
  Never lowered to hit a quota — widen the net instead. `stated`
- **Never mirror Pulsetto's named-condition claims**, however well they perform for them. `stated`
- Diversity is enforced on **angle, avatar and framework**, not on brand spread — all five briefs
  in a week may come from one competitor if that is where the winners are. `stated`

## What has worked and what has failed

Two brand-stated beliefs are **contradicted by the Q3 data**. Surface the tension rather than
resolving it silently — the brand may know something the account does not show.

- The brand states **"Yapper Founder ads" are its best format** and that it prioritises
  authenticity over polish. The Yapper tag appears **zero times** in either of the last two
  quarters, and the founder work now carries 3D graphics, a studio mic and AI voiceover.
  `stated` vs `verified` — see `audits/2026-Q3/90-day-diversity-audit.md`
- The brand states **AI ads didn't work.** The AI-voiceover founder ad HPT052 posted the
  account's best hook rate (52.43%) and hold rate (28.25%) at 1.55 ROAS. `stated` vs `verified`
- **Comparison (1.46 ROAS, $126.21 CPA) and Comment Response (1.49 ROAS) beat the account's
  1.33** while receiving almost no spend. HPT072, a Comment Response price-objection ad, is the
  only ad in the window clearing the brand's stated 2x bar. `verified`
- Three talking-head formats — Authority Figure, Educational, Founder — absorb **three quarters
  of spend and all return at or below account average**. `verified`

## Positioning and audience nuances

- **The account is handheld and electrical.** Rivals are neck-worn (Pulsetto), wrist vibration
  (Apollo), infrasonic (Sensate), earbuds (Neuvana), ear-clip (Nurosym). Truvaga is the only
  close form-factor match. Never borrow a rival's mechanism rationale. `verified`
- **Reviews say sleep, positioning says panic.** Sleep is the loudest review theme (41 of 139,
  4.88 avg), edging out anxiety/panic (37 of 139) — while the brand positions VeRelief Prime as
  the panic-ready crisis tool. Unresolved tension. `verified`
- **Three personas appear in reviews that no ICP names**: the clinician/practitioner who demos on
  patients and drives their own purchases (18 of 139, 4.44 avg), the parent regulating a child
  (18 of 139), and the chronic-illness buyer (17 of 139). Meanwhile the brand's most developed
  ICP — the first responder — produces roughly **two** identifiable reviews out of 139.
  `verified` — see `audits/2026-Q3/customer-review-audit.md`
- Working avatar names the team already uses: **HRV Hunter, Sleep Struggler, Off-ramper**.
  `stated` (`config/competitors.yml`, flagged as under-served in the brief library)
- **The category is saturated** — every vagus-nerve brand runs the same three angles. The team's
  own doctrine is that structures swiped from *outside* the category are the most valuable
  source, because no rival has used them on this audience. `stated`

## Named preferences and standing instructions

- **Ad comments are an untapped objection goldmine.** 58 "TENS unit", 50 "scam", 31 "placebo",
  98 "gel tip" — answered one reply at a time, appearing in **no creative**. Named the cheapest
  lever in the review audit. `verified`
- **The stated success bar — "2x ROAS at $1K adspend" — cannot grade an account running
  ~$11,491/day.** Until the brand supplies a real north-star metric, never present a performance
  verdict as if their definition of winning were known. `stated` / open
