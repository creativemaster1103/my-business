# Competitor snapshot — SONA.help

## The headline read

SONA is selling a product it does not appear to have shipped yet, and it is buying real delivery to do it. That is the one thing to hold about this rival. All 28 ads in its library are active, all 28 land on the same homepage at `https://sona.help/`, and every purchase line in the corpus asks for a reservation or a pre-order rather than a sale: "Reserve yours today — future you will thank you!", "Pre-order now and try it risk-free with our 30-day money-back guarantee", "Pre-order today to be among the first to own it." `verified` — that is 28 of 28 ads carrying pre-order language, read directly from the ad bodies in the Parker pull on 2026-09-08. The company is not fighting Hoolest for a customer at checkout this quarter. It is fighting Hoolest for what the category is understood to be.

The second thing to hold is what SONA has decided the category is. Nearly every ad is built on one number: heart rate variability. "How I doubled my HRV in ten minutes ⬇️" over a split screen showing 47 ms rising to 89 ms. "Guys, not to flex, but my HRV last night went up to 152," said into a phone camera over an app overlay. "Increases Heart Rate Variability (HRV) up to 2x" and "Adds 30-40 minutes of deep sleep per night" printed on a static. `verified` from the creative fields on ad archive ids 2266926160506203, 1663240495808461 and 1568869395252547. SONA is not running a stress ad or an anxiety ad. It is running a measurement ad, aimed straight at the buyer Hoolest's own context document calls the HRV Hunter — the person who trusts a wearable dashboard over their own feelings and whose fear is being fooled by placebo.

Third: the mechanism is the same family as Hoolest's, the site and the use model are not. SONA is electrical stimulation delivered at the ear through a small white device hooked over it, run through a phone app that reads heart rate variability in real time and adjusts the dose. Its own words: "Real-time HRV biofeedback", "Clinically designed, safe ear stimulation", and the branded engine "NeuroSphere™ is the intelligent engine inside SONA, adapting stimulation in real time based on your HRV, routines, and goals." `stated` — this is SONA describing itself, not a verified capability. The session it sells is ten minutes a day, every day. Hoolest sells thirty seconds in the moment it goes wrong. Those are two different products wearing the same category label, and the gap between them is the most useful thing in this document.

## Strengths, exposures, and tensions

**Genuine strengths.**

1. A single, sharp, defensible claim. Personalization is a real differentiator, it is easy to say in one line, and it cannot be disproved by a buyer in the first week. "Everybody's stressed, but nobody's nervous systems are the same" is a better sentence than anything in the other two rival libraries I read this round.
2. Borrowed authority stacked early. Press wall, tabloid feature headline, a named tech publication review, a psychotherapist review, NHS trials. For a brand with 779 Instagram followers, that is a lot of trust assembled without customers.
3. Design confidence. The product photography is clean and premium and the whole library looks like one brand. In a category full of clinical looking plastic, that reads as expensive.
4. Real creative output relative to its size. Twenty-three distinct creatives in fourteen weeks from a company this small means a working production process.

**Exposures.**

1. Nothing has shipped. Every ad asks for a pre-order. If delivery slips, the press wall becomes a liability rather than an asset.
2. No customers means no proof. There is not a single customer review, star rating, review count, or third party testimonial anywhere in the 28 ads. The strongest social proof in the library is one anonymous "early testers say" quote, re-used eight times.
3. No sensation, no demo. Twenty-eight ads and not one shows what the thing feels like or does. For an electrical device this is the gap a rival can drive straight through.
4. One destination, one ask, one persona. If the HRV Hunter turns out to be too small an audience to build a business on, this account has no second lane built.
5. Claims sitting on nothing public. "Up to 2x HRV" and "30-40 minutes of deep sleep" have no study, sample size or method attached in any ad. A skeptic with a search bar will find that quickly.

**Cross-source tensions.** With eight of nine sub-context docs missing, most tensions cannot be tested — that is the honest state of this read. Three are visible from inside the paid library alone.

- **The brand's watchlist against the live library.** Mark's note says 21 active ads and "adjacent calm/mental-health positioning" `stated`, 2026-08-29. Parker shows 28 active ads and a positioning built entirely on measured biohacking `verified`, 2026-09-07. Both cannot be right about what this company is. The library is closer to right, because it is what SONA is paying to say.
- **The claimed audience against the claimed problem.** SONA sells to a person tracking their HRV chart, then leans on a testimonial about waking at 4.30am with a knot in her stomach. Those are two different people at two different moments. The account has not decided which one it is for.
- **Institutional caution against tabloid boldness.** The same library runs "currently in NHS-supported clinical trials," which is careful and hedged, and a press headline containing the word "cured," which is not. `verified`. A brand that is genuinely inside a clinical trial process does not usually let the word "cured" run in its paid creative. One of those two postures will have to give.

## Brand-relevant implications

Hold all of these as provisional. They come from one slice of evidence, not nine.

**Where SONA leaves an opening.** The whole sensation lane is empty. SONA sells a number on a phone. Hoolest sells a feeling you get in thirty seconds, and its own customers say so in their own words: "you can feel it working." There is no demo in 28 SONA ads, no ad about what the stimulation actually feels like, no reaction, no skeptic test. If the category's noisiest new entrant is teaching buyers to expect a chart, an ad that says "forget the chart, here is what it feels like in your neck in thirty seconds" is a contrast, not an echo. `inferred`.

The second opening is the argumentative lane. SONA never runs a Comparison hook, a Myth Busting hook, or an Us versus Them static. Neither, from what I read this round, does Neuvana or Sensate. The whole category is asserting and none of it is arguing.

The third opening is the acute moment. Ten minutes a day, every day, is a routine you have to build. Hoolest's brand context already names the position no rival has claimed: the device for the moment when breathing fails. SONA's library confirms nobody is claiming it. One ad out of 28 goes near panic.

**Where SONA threatens Hoolest if left alone.** If the personalization claim sticks, "fixed dose" becomes a way to describe Hoolest that Hoolest never chose. The counter is not to add personalization. It is to make the fixed dose the point: high dose, felt immediately, no app, no Bluetooth, no calibration, works the first time. Hoolest already owns those words. They just have to be aimed at this argument before the argument sets.

The other threat is the trust ladder. SONA is assembling press, publication reviews, clinician quotes and a trials claim before it has a single customer. Hoolest has 20,000 professionals, 1,000 first responders, clinical endorsements and a real lab origin, and almost none of that appears in the tone SONA is using. The asset gap runs in Hoolest's favor and the presentation gap runs against it.

**Where Hoolest should hold rather than chase.** Do not chase the HRV number war. SONA is claiming a doubling. Hoolest's own figure is a 31% increase, which is credible and is very likely the more honest number. Trying to outbid "2x" with a real figure is a losing trade, and Hoolest's compliance flag already warns that physiological before and after assets carry platform risk. Compete on the felt sensation and the speed, where the proof is in the buyer's own hand in thirty seconds and no chart is needed.

Do not chase the aesthetic either. Hoolest's own stated philosophy is authenticity over polish, and its best performing format is a founder talking plainly. Matching SONA's premium minimal look would trade a real advantage for a fight on the rival's ground.

## Open-loops roll-up

**Loop 1 — What SONA has actually sold.**
Every one of the 28 ads asks for a pre-order or a reservation, the brand has 323 Facebook likes and 779 Instagram followers, and yet it is running enough delivery for Parker to rank 28 separate ads. That combination does not usually hold together.
*Pull: Surprise.* It fired when the follower count and the ad count landed side by side in the same record — the audience is tiny and the paid output is not.
**Question: How many people have actually put money down on SONA so far?**
Why it matters: it is the difference between a funded company buying its way into the category and a small team burning a launch budget. Hoolest would treat those two rivals very differently.
*Territory: Product.*

**Loop 2 — What the platform is letting through.**
SONA is running hard physiological numbers in live ads — a split screen of two heart rate variability readouts, "up to 2x", "30-40 minutes of deep sleep per night" — and those ads are active and ranked. Hoolest's own compliance flag warns that this exact kind of asset may face scrutiny under Meta's health and wellness rules.
*Pull: Tension.* Two sources disagree: Hoolest's internal read of the risk, and a rival running the risky asset in market without visible consequence.
**Question: What kinds of physiological claims and data screenshots are actually surviving in this category's live ads right now?**
Why it matters: if the ceiling is higher than Hoolest believes, the brand is leaving its strongest proof in a drawer. If it is not, the rival is about to lose ads and Hoolest should not copy it.
*Territory: Messaging.*

**Loop 3 — The men nobody casts.**
Across nine SONA videos the on-screen cast is six women, one anonymous man in profile, and a founder. No one on camera reads as older than about 40, and there is no first responder, shift worker, veteran or clinician anywhere in 28 ads. Hoolest's sharpest persona is a 38 year old fire captain.
*Pull: Gap.* An absence where presence would be expected — the category's own buyer base is not all young women, and yet the casting is.
**Question: Who in this category has put a man over 35 on camera, and what happened to those ads?**
Why it matters: if nobody has tried it, Hoolest has an uncontested casting lane that matches a persona it already has proof for. If several have tried it and dropped it, that is worth knowing before a shoot.
*Territory: Creators and talent.*
