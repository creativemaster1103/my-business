# Competitor snapshot — Neuvana

## The headline read

Neuvana just rebuilt its entire ad library in one week, and it used the rebuild to walk into a buyer Hoolest has never named. That is the headline.

Every one of the 18 active ads launched between 3 and 5 September 2026 `verified` — nine on the 3rd, one on the 4th, eight on the 5th, read from the launch dates on all 18 records. Nothing in the library is older than five days. A brand does not replace a whole library by accident, so the honest read is a relaunch, a new agency, or a fresh media push that started last week. `inferred`, because no running notes exist to tell me which.

What arrived with it is the interesting part. Eight of the 18 ads, 44% of the library, speak to an older woman. Four of them name menopause outright: "Menopause running your life?", "How Xen works for Menopause", "What menopause is doing to your nervous system / How 'Xen' helps restore balance", "How Xen helps Menopause Sleep issues." Four more speak to age without naming it: "The simple device helping older adults feel years younger.", "FEELING OLDER THAN YOU ARE? THIS HELPS.", "THE HIDDEN SECRET TO FEEL YOUNGER AND MORE ENERGIZED", "Why older adults are calling this their best-kept secret." `verified` from the headline fields on all 18 ads. Hoolest's five customer profiles contain no woman in the menopause years and no buyer over about 50 except by implication. A rival with the same mechanism just planted a flag in ground Hoolest has never walked on.

The third thing to hold, and it is the reason the threat is limited today: there is not one human being speaking in this entire library. Across 18 ads and three videos, nobody talks to camera, nobody tells a story, no customer appears, no founder appears, no doctor appears. The one person visible anywhere is a woman in a gym holding the device for eight seconds in a sale ad `verified`. Neuvana has 31,761 Instagram followers and 7,556 Facebook likes — the second largest owned audience of the three rivals in this round — and it is running a library of designed cards.

The research trail behind all of this: one source, the Parker competitor ad library, 18 of 18 ads analyzed, ranks stamped 2026-09-07. Eight of the nine sub-context docs are missing. Website tracking is off, so I have no price and no landing page. Everything below carries its mark.

## Threat type and brand impact

**Threat type: an established market rival running a thin creative operation. The live threat is the buyer lane it just opened, not the ads themselves.**

The market rival versus creative rival cut from `analyzing-public-ad-accounts.md` splits cleanly here. As a market rival Neuvana is real: a named product with a real audience, listed in Hoolest's own brand context as the Neuvana Xen at $449 `stated`, and carrying an Instagram following forty times the size of SONA's. As a creative rival it is currently weak. Eighteen active ads sits well under the fifty to a hundred where the same method says an account starts to look like it has cracked Meta, and 15 of the 18 are statics `verified`. There is no testing depth visible, no format range, and no talent.

But grade it in its own lane, as the method demands, and something else shows up. This is not a lazy library. The copy is sharp. "You're exhausted all day. Then wide awake at night. It's not just menopause. Your nervous system is stuck 'on.'" `verified`, ad archive id 1785905709101501. That is a real problem-aware lead written by somebody who understands the buyer. Whoever wrote that copy knows what they are doing. What is missing is everything that would carry it — a face, a voice, a story, a demo.

The impact on Hoolest runs in two directions. Right now, near zero: this account is not going to out-create Hoolest at anything. Over the next quarter, real: if the menopause lane works for Neuvana, the whole category learns that the vagus nerve device has a second buyer, and that buyer is easier to reach and less stigmatized than a first responder who will not say the word anxiety. Hoolest's own watchlist has Neuvana at `priority: low` with the note "Xen earbuds. Adjacent mechanism, overlapping avatar" `stated`, dated 2026-08-29. Nine days later, the avatar stopped overlapping. That note needs updating.

## Personas

**Who the creative is built for, in three groups.**

*Group one, the menopause buyer — 4 of 18 ads (22%).* The strongest writing in the library lives here. The lead on ad 1785905709101501 walks the symptom before it walks the product: exhausted all day, wide awake at night, then the reframe — "It's not just menopause. Your nervous system is stuck 'on.'" — and only then the mechanism, "Xen sends a gentle signal through the ear to help your body slow down again." `verified`. Ad 3474498856061972 does the same move with "Tired… but can't relax? That constant 'on' feeling isn't random." The value props Parker extracted across these four run to sleep depth, mood stability, brain fog and calm. This is problem-aware creative in the Schwartz sense, and per `killer-performance-ads.md` that is the level most brands never write down to.

*Group two, the older adult who feels old — 4 of 18 ads (22%).* Same person, one step less specific, and pitched on vitality rather than hormones: feel years younger, restore the spark, deeper sleep, natural recharging, an alternative to pills and energy drinks. `verified`. One of these headlines carries a raw percent-encoded emoji that never rendered — "Why older adults are calling this their best-kept secret. %f0%9f%92%9e%e2%9c%a8" `verified`, ad archive id 1067837152758354. Small thing, real tell: this library was built fast, from templates, with no final eye on it.

*Group three, everyone else — 10 of 18 ads (56%).* Generic calm, focus and sleep, plus the sale ads. "Relax. Recover. Refocus." "Calm your mind, Recharge your body." "Reset your Nervous System. Unlock your Potential." These are pleasant and they are not aimed at anybody in particular.

**Served against actual buyer.** The served read above is a read of who Neuvana courts. There is no buyer data on this rival — no reviews, no comments, no survey, nothing. `data-limited`. The persona method is blunt about this: a served-audience read is who the creative is built for, never who buys, and the two diverge constantly. I hold the menopause finding as a real and confident read of Neuvana's intent, and as no evidence at all about who is actually buying a Xen.

**What this means for Hoolest's own persona set.** Hoolest's five profiles are the Wired Lifer, the Off-Ramper, the Lights-Out Loser, the HRV Hunter, and Marcos the fire captain. Four of those five are men or read as men by default, and none is defined by a life stage. Note the discipline the persona method demands here: menopause is usually an **identity overlay**, not a persona, because a woman in it can also be a Lights-Out Loser or a Wired Lifer. The exception the method allows is exactly the one Neuvana is betting on — a life stage that carries its own trigger, its own message and its own buying journey. Whether it clears that bar for Hoolest is a real question, and it is one only Hoolest's own review and order data can answer.

## Product, SKU, and buyer journey

**One product, one page.** Seventeen of the 18 ads land on `https://neuvanalife.com/products/xen` and one lands on the homepage `verified`. This is a tighter funnel than SONA's, which points everything at a homepage, and a looser one than Sensate's, which builds a separate page per angle. Neuvana sends every buyer, whatever the angle that caught them, to the same product page. What that page says is unreadable — website tracking is off. `data-limited`.

**What the product is, in Neuvana's own words.** Earbuds that deliver electrical stimulation at the ear and pair it with the user's own music: "Xen combines patented vagus nerve stimulation technology with your favorite music for a simple daily wellness ritual" `stated`. Session length is 15 minutes in the sale copy and 10 minutes in the videos `verified` — a small inconsistency inside one week of creative, and the kind of thing that happens when copy comes from two places.

**The offer, and the tangle in it.** Two different discounts are live in the same library at the same time. A Labor Day sale of $50 off, named and dated, "Ends Sept. 11th!", on 4 of 18 ads by headline and one more in body copy. And "$100 off for a limited time only!" in the body of 3 further ads. `verified`, read from the ad bodies on ad archive ids 2006724990008085, 2128042558061521 and 1335814941695517 against 1091757690096730, 1762495741750080, 1759631311902767 and 2313159526160987. In total 8 of 18 ads (44%) carry a discount somewhere in their copy. The step in `analyzing-public-ad-accounts.md` on offer-led creative reads that share as one of three things: inventory clearing, near-term revenue pressure, or a substitute for a missing demand-generation engine. Given that the whole library is five days old and half of it is on sale, the third reading is the most likely, and it is `inferred`, not established.

**The buyer journey, as far as it can be read.** Cold traffic on Facebook and Instagram, one product page, one purchase, no visible lead capture, no quiz, no bundle, no subscription language anywhere in 18 ads. `verified` as an absence in the copy; the page itself is `data-limited`. Unlike Hoolest, there is no consumable in sight — no gel tips, no replacement part, no recurring cost. If that holds, it is a real advantage in the buyer's total cost of ownership, and Hoolest's own brand context already names its gel tip replacement cycle as a weakness.

## Messaging

**The claim layer is soft, and that is a choice.** Neuvana runs almost no numbers. There is no HRV figure, no percentage, no study, no sample. The strongest claim in the library is "science-backed" and "patented" `verified`. Set that beside SONA, which claims a doubling of heart rate variability, and Sensate, which claims 48% less stress in 28 days. Three rivals in one category and only one of them declines to put a number on the table. That is either caution about the claim rules or nothing to point at, and the paid library cannot tell me which. `data-limited`.

**The emotional layer is where the library is strongest.** Read against the Mindstates list in `killer-performance-ads.md`, the menopause ads run on Security and Autonomy: the body has stopped obeying and the ad offers a way to take it back. "Your nervous system has a built-in reset button, and you can press that button with Neuvana Xen" `verified`, ad archive id 2255861034952904. The vitality ads run on Esteem — feeling older than you are, wanting the spark back. The generic ads run on nothing in particular, which is the failure the same doc names: if you cannot say which emotion an ad targets, scrap it.

**The one line worth stealing, and the one worth avoiding.** Worth studying: "It's not just menopause. Your nervous system is stuck 'on.'" That is a reframe, and a reframe is the strongest kind of educational lead, because it moves the buyer from a symptom she has accepted to a mechanism she can act on. Worth avoiding: "No pills. No meditation." and "Get the STRESS-FREE life you've always wanted." The first is a step toward the medication framing Hoolest's own compliance guardrail forbids in paid creative, and the second is a promise no device can keep.

**Hooks, named properly, using `hooks.md`.** With 15 statics and 3 videos there is not much of a hook surface, but what exists is readable:

- Callout hooks carry the whole older-buyer effort: "Menopause running your life?", "FEELING OLDER THAN YOU ARE? THIS HELPS." Direct address, explicit relevance, no subtlety. This is the right move for this audience.
- Trigger Word and secret framing: "THE HIDDEN SECRET…", "…best-kept secret." A tired device, but a native one for this buyer.
- One promise hook worth pulling apart: "This is what VAGUS NERVE Stimulation feels like," running on both of the long videos (ranks 1 and 3) `verified`. It promises the sensation. Then it delivers a product shot on a rain-soaked forest or a lake at sunset with an audio waveform pulsing over it, and no person, no skin, no reaction, no face. The hook writes a cheque the ad does not cash. Under the rule in `killer-performance-ads.md` that a hook must pay off or the viewer will not buy, this is the single clearest craft failure in the library, and it is running at ranks 1 and 3.
- Offer Based leads: four ads open on the discount.

Absent across all 18: Comment Response, Myth Busting, Comparison, Scam, Reaction, Skeptic Test, Storytelling, Authority. `verified`.

**Format spread, in the `ad-formats/` vocabulary.** The library is **Graphic Video** and designed statics, with **Offer Based** and **Educational** stacked on top and a light **Aesthetic** treatment throughout. There is no **UGC Single**, no **Authority Figure**, no **Founder Ads**, no **Demo**, no **Before & After**, no **Comment Response**, no **Podcast**, no **Street Interview**, no **Testimonial** of any kind. The format spread is the narrowest of the three rivals read this round, and per the diagnostic in the public-library method, a narrow format spread is a testing ceiling.

## Talent and creators

There is nothing to read, and the nothing is the finding.

Across 18 ads: zero people speaking, zero creators, zero customers on camera, zero founders, zero experts. One human appears at all — a woman in a gym holding the black circular device in the eight-second Labor Day video `verified`, ad archive id 2313159526160987, and she does not speak. The rest of the library is product photography, nature backdrops, waveform graphics and type.

Set that against the audience Neuvana owns. 31,761 Instagram followers and 7,556 Facebook likes `verified` from the Parker brand record. An audience that size in a wellness category is normally built by people — creators, customers, a founder, a community. Something built that following, and none of it is being reused in paid. This is the organic and paid gap the public-library method warns about, and it is exactly the gap that method says cannot be asserted from the ad library alone. The organic channels audit for this rival does not exist. So I flag it as the read that needs the organic audit and stop there. `data-limited`.

Now put the casting absence next to the buyer Neuvana just chose. The older-audience method is unambiguous about what moves a buyer roughly 50 to 70: proof from people their own age, an authority or news frame carrying the credibility, a longer story that sits in the problem before the product arrives, slower cuts, big legible type. Neuvana has the callout half of that playbook and none of the format half. It has written "Menopause running your life?" and then handed the viewer a designed card. There is no woman of that age on screen anywhere in the library, no doctor, no long story, no testimonial. The gap between what this audience needs and what this library provides is the most exploitable thing about this rival.

## Strengths, exposures, and tensions

**Genuine strengths.**

1. The copy on the menopause ads is genuinely good. Problem-aware, symptom-first, then a mechanism reframe. Most brands in this category never get past feature lists.
2. A real owned audience — 31,761 on Instagram — which is an asset even if the paid library is not using it.
3. A clean funnel. Seventeen of 18 ads to the product page, no confusion about what is being sold.
4. No visible consumable. Nothing in 18 ads suggests a replacement part or a subscription, which removes an objection Hoolest has to answer.
5. Music as the ritual wrapper. Pairing the stimulation with the user's own music is a genuinely differentiated use model and it makes a daily habit easier to keep.

**Exposures.**

1. No proof of any kind. Not one review, star rating, review count, customer quote, doctor, or study figure across 18 ads `verified`. In a category where the first objection is "is this real or is it placebo," that is the whole argument left unanswered.
2. No people. The delivery system rewards creative diversity and this account has one visual idiom.
3. Nearly half the library is discounting, with two different discount amounts live in the same week.
4. The hook on the two flagship videos promises a sensation the ads never show.
5. The library has no history. Everything is five days old, so there is nothing proven in it, and the account has no evergreen ad holding a floor.

**Cross-source tensions.** With eight of nine sub-context docs missing, most cross-doc reading is not available — that is the honest state. Three tensions are visible from inside the library.

- **The brand's watchlist against the live library.** Mark's note, 2026-08-29: "Xen earbuds. Adjacent mechanism, overlapping avatar," priority low `stated`. Live library, 2026-09-07: the mechanism is not adjacent — it is the same electrical stimulation family, delivered at the ear — and the avatar stopped overlapping the week the menopause ads launched `verified`. Priority low was right in August. It is arguable now.
- **The audience against the creative.** A brand with 31,761 followers running zero human beings in paid. Either the organic audience is not made of the people the ads are chasing, or the paid team is not talking to the organic team. Both would be worth knowing and neither can be settled from here.
- **The buyer against the format.** Neuvana chose an audience that the older-audience method says converts on authority, age-matched proof and long problem-aware stories, and then built a library of short designed cards with no faces. The choice of buyer and the choice of format point in opposite directions.

## Brand-relevant implications

Provisional, all of them. One slice of evidence, not nine.

**The opening this leaves Hoolest.** Proof. Hoolest holds what Neuvana does not have a scrap of: 20,000 professionals, 1,000 first responders, named clinicians, a founder with a lab origin, and customers who write sentences like "the first time I used it I was like 'OMG my heart isn't palpitating anymore!'". If a rival is entering a proof-hungry buyer's feed with zero proof, the counter is not a better claim. It is a real person of the right age saying a real thing on camera.

The second opening is the sensation. Neuvana promised what vagus nerve stimulation feels like and then showed a waveform. Hoolest's whole differentiator, in its own customers' words, is that you can feel it working. An ad that actually delivers on that promise takes the ground Neuvana asked for and abandoned.

**The threat if left alone.** The menopause and older-adult lane is 44% of a rival's library and 0% of Hoolest's stated personas. If it works, Neuvana gets a large, reachable, less stigmatized buyer with a real trigger, while Hoolest keeps competing for the hardest customer in the category — a fire captain who will not admit he has a problem. That is not an argument for abandoning Marcos. Marcos is Hoolest's most defensible position and no rival is anywhere near it. It is an argument for finding out, from Hoolest's own reviews and orders, whether that older woman is already buying and simply has never been spoken to.

**Where Hoolest should hold rather than chase.** Do not chase the discount. Neuvana is running two overlapping discounts against a device Hoolest's own context prices at $449, while VeRelief Prime is $159. Hoolest's price is already the friendlier number, and its 60-day trial is a stronger risk reversal than a percentage off. Discounting into a rival's sale week trades margin for nothing.

Do not chase the music-and-ritual framing either. It is a genuinely nice idea and it belongs to a daily habit product. Hoolest's position is the moment it goes wrong, not the wind-down playlist, and the brand's own context is right that no rival can credibly claim the acute moment.

**A note on method.** The older-buyer half of this read was performed through the older-audience creative method in this brain, and its patterns — direct callouts, authority and news framing, age-matched proof, slower pacing, big legible type, room to sit in the problem — are what the gap in Neuvana's library is measured against. This is everything I know about advertising to older audiences.
