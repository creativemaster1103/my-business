# The modular framework

How every VeRelief script is built. This governs both skills — an originated concept and a
swiped structure are assembled the same way once the raw idea exists.

---

## 1. The gate — four things before a line gets written

**If any of the four is missing, ask. Do not start writing and do not guess one.**

| | | Decides |
|---|---|---|
| **Core desire** | which of the Life Force 8 the ad pulls on | the engine |
| **Awareness stage** | how much the viewer already knows | the **structure** |
| **Ad angle** | the argument the ad makes | the shape |
| **Persona** | who is watching | the words |

### Life Force 8

1. Survival, enjoyment of life, life extension
2. Enjoyment of food and beverages
3. Freedom from fear, pain, and danger
4. Sexual companionship
5. Comfortable living conditions
6. To be superior, winning, keeping up with the Joneses
7. Care and protection of loved ones
8. Social approval

For VeRelief the live ones are **3** (make it stop), **5** (comfort, sleep), **6** (edge,
status) and **1** (self-preservation). Desire 1 is the most dangerous of the four on compliance —
see §6.

### Awareness stages

| Stage | Where they are |
|---|---|
| Most Aware | Knows the product, wants it, hasn't bought. Name the deal. |
| Product Aware | Knows what we sell, comparing us to alternatives. Proof and differentiation. |
| Solution Aware | Wants this kind of solution, doesn't know ours does it. Lead with the result. |
| Problem Aware | Feels the problem, doesn't know a solution exists. Lead with the problem. |
| Unaware | Doesn't know they have the problem. Lead with identity or story. |

### Angles

Problem-Solution · Comparison · Transformation · Functional Benefit · Identity · Authority ·
Social Proof · Curiosity · Scarcity/Urgency · Gifting

### Personas

The four approved personas, in `verelief-prime-brief.md`. Use those, not an invented fifth.

## 2. The spine

```
HOOK  (0-3s)   structural unit — NOT composed of blocks
BODY           the container — every Product and Person block lives here
CTA            structural unit — NOT composed of blocks
```

**Product Blocks and Person Blocks are always inside the Body.** They are not three parallel
lists to draw from wherever. Structure Blocks are the frame; the other two are the contents of
one of them.

A hook may draw on the same raw material as a block — a problem moment, a scenario — but it is
not a block and is never mapped as one. Same for the CTA.

## 3. The blocks

**Product Blocks**

| Block | What it does |
|---|---|
| Product Introduction | How the product is first revealed |
| Demonstration | The product in action — usage, before/after, comparison |
| Features / Benefits | Feature → advantage → benefit |
| Buying Experience | Price, shipping, guarantee |
| Unboxing | Anticipation and trust through the packaging reveal |
| **Unique Mechanism** | **Why this works when the other thing didn't** |

**Person Blocks**

Problem Statement · Failed Alternative · Desired Result · Before & After · Social Proof ·
Storytelling

**Unique Mechanism is the highest-leverage block we have.** Every competitor in the category
runs the same three arguments; the mechanism is the only part of a script they cannot copy. If
a brief has no Unique Mechanism block, ask why before filing it.

You add blocks to the spine — you never use all of them. **Which blocks you pick is the creative
decision.** If two concepts in a run have the same block list, one of them is not a separate
concept.

## 4. Awareness decides where the product enters

This is the single most load-bearing rule in the framework. Get it wrong and nothing downstream
saves the ad.

| Stage | Product appears | Hook opens on |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | very late, or not at all | identity, story, a grabber about their life |
| Problem Aware | ~20s in | the problem, felt |
| Solution Aware | early | the result they want |
| Product Aware | immediately | the comparison |
| Most Aware | first frame | the offer |

The same concept at two stages is two different scripts, not one script with a different hook.

## 5. Hooks

**Four hooks per brief, and all four sit at the same awareness level as the brief.**

### A hook is 3-4 seconds. Maximum 10 words.

That is the whole budget. At normal VO pace, 3 seconds is about 8 words and 4 seconds is about
10 — so a hook has room for one idea stated once, and nothing else.

What that rules out, every time:

- **Set-up.** No clause that exists to make the next clause make sense.
- **A second clause explaining the first.** If the hook needs explaining it is not a hook. Cut
  the explanation and let the silence do the work.
- **Anything that isn't in the persona's own register.** A trades-worker Wired Lifer speaks in
  flat, clipped declaratives; a hook written in marketing cadence gets skipped no matter how
  accurate it is. Write the words they would actually use about themselves.

Count the words before filing. A 15-word hook is not a hook that runs slightly long — it is a
hook whose first 10 words have to carry a viewer who has already gone.

Four ways into **one** concept: same body, same CTA, same persona, same `HPT` number, four
different openings. Not four concepts.

They must be four distinct **mechanisms**, not four rewordings:

| Mechanism | What it does in the first second |
|---|---|
| Reframe | Tells them the thing they believe is wrong |
| Cost | Names what the problem is costing them |
| In medias res | Drops them mid-moment, no setup |
| Direct callout | Names the person: "If you've ever…" |
| Question | Makes them answer something about themselves |
| Pattern interrupt | A visual or tonal jolt that does not belong on the feed |

**The alignment test.** A hook is aligned when it *could only have been written for that stage*:

- A **Problem Aware** hook that gestures at a solution, product, category or mechanism has
  drifted to Solution Aware. Disqualified.
- A **Problem Aware** hook that never names or dramatises the problem has drifted to Unaware.
  Disqualified.
- A **Solution Aware** hook that explains a problem they already accept is wasting the opening.
- A **Product Aware** hook that isn't differentiation is the wrong hook.

Audit all four against the stage before filing. This is not advisory — the awareness letter is
in every file name (see `naming-convention.md`), so a mismatch is visible to everyone
downstream.

If a concept cannot carry four distinct openings at its stage, **it is the wrong concept.** Drop
it and take the next one rather than shipping a short brief.

## 6. Where this collides with compliance

Run the gate in `verelief-prime-brief.md` on every script **and on all four hooks**. The hook is
where an unapproved claim hides best, because it is written last and read first.

**Desire 1 (survival / life extension) needs its own rule.** It is the strongest motivator we
have for Wired Lifer — their real fear is that chronic stress is physically damaging them — and
it sits one sentence away from a disease-prevention claim.

> **The fear belongs to the character. The claim belongs to the product. Never weld them
> together.**

A character may wonder what years of running hot is costing them. The product then delivers a
felt downshift. The script must never connect the two — *"so I got this, and now I'm not worried
about my heart"* is a disease claim and it kills the ad. Anyone "tightening" a script by joining
those beats has broken it.

The same discipline, less acutely, applies to desires 3 and 5: name the feeling, never the
condition.
