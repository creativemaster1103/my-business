# The 10 general clips

Every UGC run ships **2 scripts and 10 general clips**. The clips are captured once, in the same
shoot, and cut into **either** script.

## What "general" means

A general clip is script-agnostic: it carries no spoken line, no framework-specific staging, and
nothing that ties it to one concept. The test is blunt —

> **If it cannot be cut into both scripts, it is not a general clip.** It is script b-roll, and it
> does not count toward the ten.

That constraint is the point. It forces the ten toward the visual beats that work for *any*
VeRelief argument — the device, the state before, the state after, the thing they tried instead —
which are exactly the beats a talking head cannot carry on its own.

## Why ten, and why they matter more than the script

A UGC script lives or dies in the edit, and the edit is limited by what was captured. Two scripts
shot as pure talking head give the editor nothing to cut to, so they cut on jump cuts, which reads
as an ad. Ten general clips turn two 40-second monologues into two edits with visual rhythm — and
they cost about fifteen minutes at the end of a shoot that was happening anyway.

They also **accumulate**. Clips from previous runs stay usable, because nothing in them is tied to
a concept. After a few shoots there is a library, and a script can be built around footage that
already exists.

## The ten

| # | Section | Clip | Cuts in at | Why it earns its place |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | `Device` | **Device close in hand**, unbranded surface, slow turn | Hook | The object reveal. Shows the product before anything explains it — curiosity beats exposition, and it is the single most reusable frame in the bank |
| **2** | `Application` | **Device applied correctly**, second angle, no face | Body | The mechanism beat. Every script has a moment where the viewer needs to see *how*. Shot without a face so it cuts under any script |
| **3** | `Struggle` | **The before state** — jaw set, shoulders up, staring past the screen. No device in frame | Hook | The recognition beat. The frame that makes the right viewer stop, and it works under any hook because it is a state, not a story |
| **4** | `Relief` | **The exhale.** Shoulders drop. Held long, no cut | Body | The payoff, shown rather than claimed. Useful under the gates too: a body settling is an observable moment, not an outcome claim |
| **5** | `Alternatives` | **The failed alternative in frame** — cold tea, wine glass, meditation app open | Body | The objection beat. Pre-empts "I already tried something" without a word spoken. Stage it honestly, and never as a named medication |
| **6** | `Portability` | **Device out of a bag, pocket or drawer**, one motion | Body | Portability proved instead of asserted. Answers "when would I actually use this" in one second |
| **7** | `Trigger` | **The trigger on screen** — 3am phone, packed calendar, unread inbox | Hook | The situation beat. Names *when* the problem happens without naming a condition, which is the line the compliance gate cares about |
| **8** | `Gel tip` | **Gel tip detail**, macro, texture | Body | The consumable, and the only genuinely tactile frame in the set. Also the repeat-purchase engine, so worth having on hand |
| **9** | `Everyday` | **Hands doing something ordinary nearby** — laptop, kettle, phone face-down | Any | The connective tissue. Unglamorous, endlessly cuttable, and the clip that saves an edit with an awkward seam in it |
| **10** | `Aftermath` | **Back to it** — returns to the desk or the bed, settled. No device in frame | CTA | The close. Life resumes, undramatically. The tonal opposite of a transformation shot, which is what keeps it on-brand |

**The `Section` column is not decoration.** It is what the creator names the file after —
`BRoll_Struggle`, `BRoll_Gel tip` — because the brief's naming block says *"the name of 'section'
can be found in the Shotlist above"*. Put it at the front of the `Visual description` cell, in
bold, then the description. A numbered row with no section name gives the creator nothing to name
the file after.

## Capture notes

- **Shoot every clip twice**, once wide-ish and once close. Costs nothing, doubles the edit's options.
- **Hold each one for 6–8 seconds.** Editors need handles. A 2-second clip is unusable.
- **No talking in any of them.** The moment a clip has a spoken line it belongs to one script.
- **Keep the same room and wardrobe as the scripts** — a clip shot in a different location or
  outfit will not cut into either, which defeats the purpose.
- **Available light, hand-held, same phone.** They have to match the talking-head footage.
- **C03, C04 and C10 are performances, not b-roll.** Direct them: give the creator a real thing to
  think about rather than asking them to look stressed. A performed sigh is visible instantly.

## Compliance applies to clips too

Footage makes claims. Run the ten through the same gates:

- **C05** stages a *failed alternative*. A visible prescription bottle implies the device replaces
  medication — cut it. Cold tea, a wine glass, an app: fine.
- **C04 and C10** show a settled state. That is a feeling state, which is ours to show. Do not
  caption them with an outcome the gate would block.
- **C02** must show **correct** placement. A clip of wrong use is worse than no clip, and it
  outlives the script it was shot for.
- No white coats, clinical set dressing or medical props in any clip — see the endorsement gate in
  `ugc-creator-brief.md`.

## In the brief

The ten go into the **`B-ROLLS | SHOTLIST`** table on the brief page, one clip per row:

| Shotlist | Visual description | INSPO |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Device** — device close in hand, unbranded surface, slow turn. Hold 6-8s | *(link, if any)* |

The template ships with **8** rows. Extend it to **10**. The row count is not sacred; the ten
clips are.

`INSPO` takes a reference link when one exists and stays empty otherwise — an invented reference
is worse than a blank cell.

One shotlist serves **both scripts on the page**. The creator shoots the ten once; both edits cut
from the same set. Do not split the ten between Script 1 and Script 2, and do not write a second
shotlist.

Clips are **raw footage, not deliverable cuts.** They get no `HPT` number and no naming-sheet row
— that sheet belongs to the AI-VO pipeline and is not used in this database at all. The creator
names them `BRoll_<section>` straight off the shotlist.
