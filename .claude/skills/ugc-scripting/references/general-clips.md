# The 10 general clips

Every UGC brief ships **2 scripts and 10 general clips**. The clips are captured once, in the same
shoot, and cut into **either** script.

## One person, one iPhone, no crew

**This is the constraint that decides everything below.** The creators are not running a
production shoot. They have a phone, the room they live in, and about fifteen minutes. A clip they
cannot physically execute alone comes back missing, and nobody finds out until the edit.

Every clip on this list obeys all of these. Any replacement must too:

- **One person, one iPhone.** No second camera operator, no second phone, no tripod, no gimbal,
  no lighting kit, no macro lens.
- **Two hands, or a propped phone — never both hands and handheld.** If the clip needs both of
  the creator's hands, the phone gets **propped against a stack of books, a mug, or a wall**. Say
  which in the brief. The `Phone` column below is not optional detail.
- **Nothing to buy.** Only the device, the gel tips, and things already in their home.
- **One continuous take, 6–8 seconds.** No choreography, no timing to hit, no second angle within
  a take.
- **No editing.** They send raw clips. No filters, no colour grading, no speed ramps.
- **Nothing on a phone screen** — they are filming *with* their phone. A screen shot means a
  laptop, a TV, or a wall calendar.

> **The test:** could they shoot it, alone, in their kitchen, in under a minute, without moving
> furniture or asking anyone for help? If not, it is the wrong clip.

## What "general" means

A general clip is script-agnostic: no spoken line, no framework-specific staging, nothing tying it
to one concept. The test is blunt —

> **If it cannot be cut into both scripts, it is not a general clip.** It is script b-roll, and it
> does not count toward the ten.

That constraint is the point. It forces the ten toward the visual beats that work for *any*
Hoolest argument — the device, the state before, the state after, the thing they tried instead —
which are exactly the beats a talking head cannot carry on its own.

## Why ten, and why they matter more than the script

A UGC script lives or dies in the edit, and the edit is limited by what was captured. Two scripts
shot as pure talking head give the editor nothing to cut to, so they cut on jump cuts, which reads
as an ad. Ten simple clips turn two 40-second monologues into two edits with visual rhythm — and
they cost about fifteen minutes at the end of a shoot that was happening anyway.

They also **accumulate**. Clips from previous shoots stay usable, because nothing in them is tied
to a concept. After a few shoots there is a library.

## The ten — 7 block, 3 freestyle

**Seven clips are built from the modular framework**, so each has a known place in the script and
the editor knows which beat it cuts under. **Three are deliberately open** — the creator's own
call. See `modular-framework.md` for the blocks.

### The seven block clips

| Shotlist | Block | Clip — as the creator reads it | Phone |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Product Intro** | Product Introduction | Hold the Mini in one hand over a plain table. Turn it over slowly. | handheld |
| **Product Demo** | Demonstration | Use the Mini just under your ear, face out of frame. | **propped** |
| **Product Feature** | Features/Benefits | Reach into a bag on the table and pull the Mini out. One motion. | handheld |
| **Person Problem** | Problem Statement | Sit how you actually sit at 4pm — shoulders up, jaw tight. Do nothing for 8 seconds. No device in frame. | **propped** |
| **Person Failed Alt** | Failed Alternative | Point the phone at what you tried before — cold tea, a meditation app, a yoga mat. | handheld |
| **Person Desired Result** | Desired Result | Same setup as Person Problem. Let one long breath out, shoulders drop. | **propped** |
| **Person Before & After** | Before & After *(situational only)* | Walk back to the desk or get into bed, settled. No device in frame. | **propped** |

### The three freestyle clips

| Shotlist | Clip — as the creator reads it |
|---|---|
| **Free 1** | Your space — somewhere this script would actually happen for you. Your call what that is. |
| **Free 2** | Anything you think fits. Something you'd film for your own feed. |
| **Free 3** | A moment from your day. Doesn't have to be about the product. |

**Why three are open.** A fully specified shotlist gets you exactly what you asked for and nothing
better. The creator knows their own room, their own routine and what reads as real on their feed;
we do not. These three slots are where the clip you did not think to ask for comes from, and in
practice they are often the ones that cut.

**Freestyle still obeys the rules.** No talking, 6–8 seconds, same room and clothes, shot alone on
the phone. And the compliance gates still apply to footage: **no medication or pill bottles in
frame, no clinical or medical set dressing, nothing staging an episode.** Say that on the row if
there is any doubt — it is easier than re-shooting.

**The `Shotlist` cell is the file name.** `Product Intro` → `BRoll_ProductIntro`, `Free 1` →
`BRoll_Free1`. Short and unique, which is why a block used twice would have to be numbered — and
why we cut the duplicates rather than number them.

**Blocks deliberately not shot:** Social Proof (a creator citing numbers is a claim the brand
owns), Buying Experience (no price while Shopify is on hold), Unboxing and Storytelling (a
different shoot — and Storytelling is what the freestyle slots tend to produce anyway).

**Keep descriptions to one or two plain sentences**, plus whether to prop the phone. A creator
skims this on set; a paragraph does not get read.

## Brief mode changes how they are written

The ten clips are the same ten either way. How specifically you describe them is not.

- **Creator-specific** — stage them in the creator's actual space. `Struggle` is *their* desk at
  4pm. Specific beats generic, and you know enough to be specific.
- **General** — describe them so **anyone** can shoot them. No named rooms, no assumed props. If a
  clip only works in one person's home, it is not general and the brief comes back half-shot.

Either way the execution rules hold. Specific does not mean elaborate.

## Capture notes

- **One good take each.** Do not ask for a wide and a close of everything — that doubles a
  fifteen-minute job and the second version rarely gets used. Reshoot only what is unusable.
- **Hold 6–8 seconds.** Editors need handles. A 2-second clip is unusable.
- **No talking in any of them.** The moment a clip has a spoken line it belongs to one script.
- **Same room and clothes as the talking-head footage** — a clip shot in a different location or
  outfit will not cut into either script, which defeats the purpose.
- **Clips 3, 4 and 10 are the hard ones**, because they are performances rather than objects.
  Direct them by giving the creator something real to think about, not by asking them to look
  stressed. A performed sigh is visible instantly. If one comes back over-acted, the rest of the
  bank still works — which is part of why there are ten.

## Compliance applies to clips too

Footage makes claims. Run the ten through the same gates:

- **`Alternatives`** stages a *failed alternative*. A visible prescription bottle implies the
  device replaces medication — cut it. Cold tea, a wine glass, an app: fine.
- **`Relief`** and **`Aftermath`** show a settled state. That is a feeling state, which is ours to
  show. Do not caption them with an outcome the gate would block.
- **`Application`** must show **correct** placement. A clip of wrong use is worse than no clip, and
  it outlives the script it was shot for.
- No white coats, clinical set dressing or medical props in any clip — see the endorsement gate in
  `ugc-creator-brief.md`.

## In the brief

The ten go into the **`B-ROLLS | SHOTLIST`** table, one clip per row:

| Shotlist | Visual description | INSPO |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Device** — hold the device in one hand over a plain table, turn it over slowly once. Handheld. Hold 6–8s | *(link, if any)* |

The template ships with **8** rows. Extend it to **10**. The row count is not sacred; the ten
clips are.

`INSPO` takes a reference link when one exists and stays empty otherwise — an invented reference
is worse than a blank cell.

One shotlist serves **both scripts on the page**. The creator shoots the ten once; both edits cut
from the same set. Do not split the ten between Script 1 and Script 2, and do not write a second
shotlist.

Clips are **raw footage, not deliverable cuts.** They get no `HPT` number and no naming-sheet row
— that sheet belongs to the AI-VO pipeline and is not used in this database at all. The creator
names them `BRoll_<section>` straight off the shotlist.
